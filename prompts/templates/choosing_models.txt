Method: choosing models for a task (pictures, video, voice, text) - cheap but capable enough. A preferred way of working - adapt it to the request.

1. Say what each task needs, in plain terms: e.g. pictures in a given style; the same characters across many pictures (how many reference pictures go in); editing an existing picture; lettering in the picture; picture to video; first / last frame; a voice in a given language.
2. Shortlist from facts, not names: what is installed here (list_models: its card, its code, how long it took here) and on the services (list_online_models / search_online_models: its inputs from the service's schema, its price from the service's own page). Public quality rankings (e.g. Artificial Analysis arenas) and the official pages help when Web search is on. Check the licence when the work may be used commercially - a paid service usually covers the use of what it makes, a downloaded model carries its own licence.
3. Bake-off before a big batch: render 3-5 items typical of the real project on 2-4 candidates, the same inputs for each. A judge (another model family than yours where possible) scores them against the task's checklist; record seconds and cost per item. Show the user the results and the choice, with why.
4. Tier by role: drafts and story-reel frames on the cheapest fast model (a local one is free); finals on the model that won the bake-off; paid models only where they are clearly better or nothing local can do the job (video, a language no local voice speaks) - say why and the cost.
5. Models and prices change fast: don't rely on an old choice - repeat the bake-off for a new project when the needs differ or months have passed, and note the results in the project folder for next time.
